# University Academic Policies — Computer Science Program

*Effective Fall 2024 | Last Updated: August 2026*

---

## §1 Prerequisites and Waivers

### §1.1 Prerequisite Enforcement

All course prerequisites must be completed with a minimum grade of **D** (1.0) unless a higher grade is specified in the course description. Prerequisites are automatically enforced at the time of registration. Students who do not meet the prerequisite requirements will be blocked from enrolling.

Concurrent enrollment in a prerequisite course is only permitted where explicitly noted in the course catalog. Students must maintain enrollment in the concurrent prerequisite through the end of the semester; dropping the concurrent prerequisite requires dropping the dependent course as well.

### §1.2 Prerequisite Waivers

Students may petition for a prerequisite waiver under the following conditions:

1. **Demonstrated competency**: The student can demonstrate equivalent knowledge through professional experience, self-study, or coursework at another institution not eligible for transfer credit.
2. **Instructor approval**: The course instructor must provide written approval for the waiver.
3. **Department chair signature**: The department chair must co-sign all prerequisite waiver requests.

Waiver petitions must be submitted at least **two weeks before the start of the semester**. Approved waivers are valid for one semester only and do not carry over.

### §1.3 Minimum Grade Requirements

Certain advanced courses require a minimum grade higher than D in prerequisite courses. These requirements are specified in the course catalog and cannot be waived. Courses with elevated grade requirements include:

- CS201 Data Structures requires **C** or higher in CS102
- CS301 Algorithms requires **C** or higher in CS201
- CS303 Operating Systems requires **C** or higher in CS201
- MATH102 Calculus II requires **C** or higher in MATH101

## §2 Course Substitutions

### §2.1 Equivalent Courses

The department maintains an official list of course equivalencies. Courses designated as equivalent may be substituted for one another without additional approval. The current equivalency list is maintained by the Registrar's Office.

### §2.2 Substitution by Petition

Students may petition to substitute a non-equivalent course for a degree requirement under the following conditions:

1. The substitute course covers at least **75% of the learning outcomes** of the required course.
2. The substitute course has equal or greater credit hours.
3. The student provides a written justification including syllabi comparison.
4. The academic advisor and department chair approve the petition.

Substitution petitions require a **minimum 3-week processing period**. Approved substitutions are recorded on the student's degree audit.

### §2.3 Cross-Listed Courses

Cross-listed courses may only be counted once toward degree requirements. Students may choose which department listing to apply the credit under, but this decision is final once the course is completed.

## §3 Transfer Credits

### §3.1 Acceptance of Transfer Credits

Transfer credits from regionally accredited institutions are evaluated by the Registrar's Office. The following criteria apply:

1. Courses must have been completed with a grade of **C** (2.0) or higher.
2. Courses must be from a regionally accredited institution.
3. Credits must be equivalent in content and level to the university's courses.
4. A maximum of **60 transfer credits** may be applied toward the 120-credit degree requirement.
5. Transfer credits are recorded as "P" (Pass) and do not affect the university GPA.

### §3.2 Transfer Credit for Core Requirements

Transfer credits may satisfy core CS requirements only if the course content aligns with at least **80% of the university course syllabus**. The department chair must approve transfer credit for all 300-level and above CS courses.

### §3.3 AP and IB Credit

Advanced Placement (AP) scores of 4 or 5 are accepted for equivalent courses. International Baccalaureate (IB) Higher Level scores of 5, 6, or 7 are accepted. Specific equivalencies are published annually by the Registrar.

## §4 Academic Standing

### §4.1 Good Standing

Students are in good academic standing when maintaining a cumulative GPA of **2.0** or higher and a major GPA of **2.5** or higher.

### §4.2 Academic Probation

Students whose cumulative GPA falls below **2.0** or whose major GPA falls below **2.5** are placed on academic probation. Students on probation:

1. Must meet with their academic advisor within the first two weeks of the semester.
2. Are limited to a maximum course load of **14 credits** per semester.
3. May not enroll in courses with a difficulty level of 4 or higher.
4. Must achieve a semester GPA of at least **2.5** to be removed from probation.

### §4.3 Academic Dismissal

Students who fail to meet probation requirements for **two consecutive semesters** are subject to academic dismissal. Dismissed students may appeal through the Academic Standards Committee.

## §5 Credit Load Limits

### §5.1 Standard Course Load

The standard course load is **15 credits** per semester. The maximum standard load is **18 credits** per semester.

### §5.2 Overload Requests

Students wishing to enroll in more than 18 credits must:

1. Have a cumulative GPA of **3.5** or higher.
2. Have no incomplete ("I") grades from previous semesters.
3. Obtain written approval from their academic advisor.
4. Receive final approval from the department chair.
5. The absolute maximum is **21 credits** per semester.

### §5.3 Minimum Credit Load

Full-time enrollment requires a minimum of **12 credits** per semester. Students enrolled in fewer than 12 credits are classified as part-time, which may affect financial aid, housing, and visa status.

## §6 Repeat and Grade Replacement Policy

### §6.1 Course Repetition

Students may repeat a course in which they received a grade of **C-** or lower, or a grade of **W** (Withdrawal). The following rules apply:

1. A course may be repeated a maximum of **two times** (three total attempts).
2. Both the original and repeat grades appear on the transcript.
3. Only the **highest grade** is used in GPA calculation (grade replacement).
4. Credit hours are counted only once.

### §6.2 Grade Replacement Limitations

Grade replacement is limited to a maximum of **four courses** (12 credit hours) during a student's academic career. Courses repeated beyond the grade replacement limit will have all attempts included in the GPA calculation.

### §6.3 Withdrawal Policy

Students may withdraw from a course through the **10th week** of the semester with a grade of "W". Withdrawals after the 10th week require documented extenuating circumstances and dean approval. A maximum of **3 withdrawals** per academic year is permitted.

## §7 Graduation Requirements

### §7.1 Credit Requirements

To graduate with a B.S. in Computer Science, students must complete:

1. A minimum of **120 total credit hours**.
2. Core CS courses: minimum **33 credits**.
3. Mathematics: minimum **18 credits**.
4. Natural Sciences: minimum **6 credits**.
5. Laboratory: minimum **2 credits**.
6. General Education: minimum **21 credits**.
7. CS Electives: minimum **12 credits** (at least 4 elective courses).
8. Senior Capstone: **3 credits**.

### §7.2 GPA Requirements

1. Cumulative GPA: minimum **2.0**.
2. Major (CS + MATH) GPA: minimum **2.5**.
3. No unresolved incomplete grades.

### §7.3 Residency Requirement

Students must complete at least **30 credit hours** at the university, including at least **18 credit hours** of upper-division (300-level and above) CS coursework.

### §7.4 Time Limit

All degree requirements must be completed within **8 years** of initial enrollment. Courses completed more than 8 years prior to graduation may need to be retaken if the content has significantly changed.

### §7.5 Graduation Application

Students must apply for graduation by the posted deadline (typically 3 months before commencement). Late applications may delay diploma conferral.

## §8 Double Counting Policy

### §8.1 General Rule

A single course may satisfy requirements in **at most two** different categories. For example, PHIL301 Ethics in Technology can count toward both General Education and the Ethics requirement.

### §8.2 Restrictions

1. A course cannot be double-counted for both a major requirement and a minor requirement in the same department.
2. Capstone credits cannot be double-counted for elective credits.
3. Lab courses cannot be counted toward the General Education requirement.

### §8.3 Credit Hour Counting

When a course is double-counted, its credit hours are applied to each satisfied category but counted only **once** toward the 120-credit total.
